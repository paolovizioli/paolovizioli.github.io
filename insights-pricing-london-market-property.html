Pricing London Market property: from theory to a working model

Paolo Vizioli · 11 November 2026

Last week I announced our HX Renew pricing model implementation programme, which started with the Property class of business. Here's a closer look at what we built and why.

1. The theory in brief

Direct and facultative (D&F) commercial property business in the London Market is hard to price from a client's own claims. Each risk is large and unlike the others. Even a big insured produces only a handful of losses over a decade, and almost none in the upper layers being written. So the market relies on exposure rating:

  • apply a ground-up loss rate per unit of insured value, drawn from a large book of business rather than from the single risk;
  • use an exposure curve to share that expected loss across the deductible, limit and layers;
  • blend in the insured's own experience, insofar as available, weighted by how far it can be trusted;
  • price catastrophe separately with catastrophe models, because past claims are a poor guide to rare, severe events;
  • add expenses, a cost of capital / profit margin and a limited underwriter adjustment to reach the technical premium; the actual premium is then assessed against the technical premium on a portfolio basis to determine price adequacy.

2. The model: structure and mechanics

Our Property model follows that chain, and every step is visible:

  • Location-level rating: each location is rated separately for property damage, business interruption and machinery breakdown, using rates by occupancy class.
  • Exposure curves: a single parameter adjusts the shape of the claim-size curve to the class of risk.
  • Claim frequency from the curve itself: the model splits expected losses into attritional, large and very large bands, so frequency and severity can be seen separately.
  • Two ways of weighting experience: a Bayesian update for large losses and classical credibility for attritional ones.
  • Tower first, then layer: experience is calibrated on the full tower, where the claims data sits, and then applied to the layer being priced.
  • Catastrophe load: the model is connected to an external catastrophe model, which provides the catastrophe load as a separate component of the price.
  • Three technical prices side by side: exposure only, with experience, and after limited underwriter adjustments. Each is grossed up to the combined ratio targets.

3. The model against the theory

The mechanics follow the textbook closely, with the exposure curve, frequency extraction and credibility built as the theory sets them out, and catastrophe priced separately from the exposure rating. That matters because a price everyone can trace back to recognised theory is easier to challenge and to trust.

The next stages of the programme build on that foundation:

  • enriching the experience module with claims data adjusted to the rating year;
  • refining the loadings for reinsurance, capital and investment income.

A model is only as good as its data and assumptions. We're building ours step by step, checking each one against the theory, so that underwriters and actuaries can see exactly how a price is reached.
